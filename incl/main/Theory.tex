\chapter{Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers}

% The collection of all $k$-mers is represented as
% \begin{align*}
% T = (t_1, t_2, \ldots, t_{L - k + 1}) \in \R^{k \times L-k+1},
% \end{align*}

This chapter focuses on the architecture of DNABERT, a pre-trained Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers model designed for genomic sequences, see Figure \ref{fig: architecture DNAbert}. DNABERT extends the original BERT framework first developed for natural language processing by replacing words with DNA k-mers, enabling the model to learn representations of nucleotide sequences, that can be used for predictive genomic tasks.

Initially, the chapter begins by describing how raw DNA sequences are divided into overlapping k-mers, which serve as the fundamental input units. It then explains the masking procedure used during pre-training, where a subset of k-mers is hidden to force the model to infer them from surrounding context. Next, the construction of token, positional, and segment embeddings is presented. These embeddings encode the k-mers before being passed into the transformer architecture, which is composed of stacked sublayers including multi-head self-attention, feed-forward networks, and layer normalization. The resulting hidden states are then examined, both in their role for sequence-level classification (predicting global properties of entire DNA sequences) and for token-level classification (recovering masked k-mers during pre-training).

The chapter then examins the training process. The pre-training phase is outlined, showing how the model learns generalizable representations of DNA sequences. The chapter also details how a pre-trained DNABERT can be fine-tuned for specific predictive tasks.

Finally, the chapter introduces the evaluation metrics used to assess model performance. Standard metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC curves are described, along with their relevance for both sequence-level and token-level tasks.

\section{Input sequence}
Let $\Sigma = \{ A, T, G, C \}$ denote the nucleotide alphabet, and let an ordered nucleotide sequence be defined as
\begin{align*}
S = (s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_L), \quad s_i \in \Sigma, \; \forall i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L\}, L \in \N,
\end{align*}
such that $S \in \Sigma^L$ represents the set of all possible nucleotide strings of length $L$. To prepare an arbitrary nucleotide sequence, $S$, for input to the DNABERT model, the sequence is first split into overlapping $k$-mers defined as
\begin{align*}
t_i = (s_i, s_{i+1}, \ldots, s_{i+k-1}), \quad \forall i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, L - k + 1\}.
\end{align*}
The sequence of these $k$-mers, $T = (t_1, t_2, \dots, t_{L-k+1})$, forms the primary component of the model's input. The complete input representation for DNABERT integrates the sequence of $k$-mers with five special tokens
\begin{align*}
    K = ([CLS], k_1, k_2, \dots, k_{L-k+1}, [SEP]),
\end{align*}
where $k_i = \{t_i, [PAD], [MASK], [UNK] \}$ and each representation of the special tokens are summarized in Table \ref{tab:special_tokens}.

\begin{align*}
S' = [\text{CLS}],\, t_1, t_2, \ldots, t_{L-k+1}, [\text{SEP}],
\end{align*}
where $(\text{CLS}])$ denotes a classification token appended at the start of the sequence, and $[\text{SEP}]$ marks its termination. 
Each element in $S'$ is subsequently embedded through the token, positional, and (if applicable) segment embedding layers before being passed into the transformer encoder.
